Cloud & Virtualization Solutions

Move the parts of your IT that suit a hosted service, virtualise what is better kept close, and leave in place whatever is working and not worth the upheaval.

What this covers

Moving to the cloud is often sold as a decision you make once. In practice it is a series of small ones: which systems genuinely belong in a hosted service, and which are fine where they are. We work that out with you rather than moving everything because it is fashionable.

Where hardware is the weak point, several virtual servers can run on one physical machine, so a failing disk is no longer the end of the working week. Email and files can move to a hosted service with everyone's devices set up on it. Remote access can let people work from somewhere else without opening the whole network up, and backup can cover the data that no longer sits in your building.

What's included

How a move to cloud or virtualisation is planned and carried out.

  • A review of what you run, and which parts genuinely suit a hosted service
  • Several virtual servers on one physical machine, so old hardware stops being the weak point
  • Email and files moved to a hosted service, with everyone's devices set up on it
  • Remote access that lets people work from somewhere else without opening the whole network up
  • Backup and retention for the data that no longer sits in your building
  • A review of what the monthly bills are actually for

How we work

The same four steps whether it is one laptop or a whole site.

  1. 1

    Tell us about it

    By phone, email or the form on the contact page. We confirm it has been picked up, and say who has it.

  2. 2

    We diagnose it

    Remote first where that is quicker, on site where it is not. Either way, you are told what we found.

  3. 3

    We fix it

    The fix, why it happened, and what to do if it comes back — in language that does not need a glossary.

  4. 4

    We stop it coming back

    Where the same fault keeps returning, we look at the cause rather than the symptom, and tell you what it would take.

Why it works

What you get out of moving some things and leaving others alone.

Nothing moves by habit

Each system is looked at on its own merits, so a move is chosen for a reason rather than because everyone else is doing it.

Hardware stops being the weak point

Virtual servers can be moved off a failing machine, and hosted services keep running while the box in the corner is replaced.

Costs you can see

Hosted services are billed monthly, so knowing what those bills are for is part of the work rather than an afterthought.

Questions we get asked

Does everything have to move to the cloud?

No. Some systems are better off where they are, and moving them would cost time and money for no real gain. Working out which is which is the first part of the job.

What happens to our data if we stop working with you?

The data is yours, and you get it back. We hand over the accounts, the files and the settings, and we take our own access away once you have confirmed the handover is complete.

Will staff notice the change?

Usually less than they expect. Where a change alters how people work — a different way to reach email or files, for instance — everyone is shown it first and told who to ask if something is not where they left it.

Is cloud more expensive than our own servers?

Sometimes it is, sometimes it is not, and it depends on what you run and how hard you run it. A monthly bill is easier to predict; a machine doing very little can be cheaper to keep.

What if we move something and it does not work out?

Then it can be brought back, and it is worth saying up front what that would involve. A move that cannot be undone is one we would rather not make in the first place.
