Cybersecurity & Network Security

Closing the easy ways into your business: the firewall and what is allowed through it, updates on the devices people use, sign-in that asks for more than a password, and backups you have restored from.

What this covers

Most of what reaches a business is not sophisticated. It is a login page that looks about right, an invoice whose bank details have changed, a laptop that is a version behind. None of that needs a skilled attacker. It needs one person having a busy morning.

So the work is on the ordinary things: the firewall and what you let through it, updates and protection on the devices your team actually uses, and a second factor behind the password. Staff are taught what a suspicious email looks like, access is removed when someone leaves, and backups are restored as a test. It is unglamorous, and it is what most incidents turn on.

What's included

The practical work of making your business harder to walk into.

  • Firewall rules reviewed, so only the traffic you need gets through
  • Updates and protection kept current on the devices people use
  • Multi-factor sign-in for email, remote access and your key systems
  • Staff sessions on what a suspicious message looks like, and tests after them
  • Access removed properly when someone leaves or changes role
  • Backups restored as a test, because that is what makes them real

How we work

The same four steps whether it is one laptop or a whole site.

  1. 1

    Tell us about it

    By phone, email or the form on the contact page. We confirm it has been picked up, and say who has it.

  2. 2

    We diagnose it

    Remote first where that is quicker, on site where it is not. Either way, you are told what we found.

  3. 3

    We fix it

    The fix, why it happened, and what to do if it comes back — in language that does not need a glossary.

  4. 4

    We stop it coming back

    Where the same fault keeps returning, we look at the cause rather than the symptom, and tell you what it would take.

Why it works

What you get out of treating security as routine work.

Fewer ways in

The firewall, the updates and the sign-in controls are looked at together, so the gaps between them are closed rather than only the ones in front of you.

Backups you can trust

A restore is run as a test, so you find out whether the backup works on an ordinary afternoon rather than in the middle of an incident.

Staff who know what to look for

People are the target of most attempts, so they are shown what a suspicious message looks like and then tested on it, as a team picture.

Questions we get asked

Is this only worth doing for large businesses?

No. Small businesses are targeted because a single stolen password can stop a week of trading, and because nobody expects it to happen to them. The work is scaled to what you actually run, rather than to a template built for someone else.

What is multi-factor authentication and do we have to use it?

It means proving who you are with something beyond a password — a code from an app, a prompt on your phone, or a small hardware key. We put it on the accounts that would cause the most trouble if they were taken: email, remote access and anything holding client or financial data.

What do we do if we think we have been breached?

Tell us first, before you start tidying up, because the order of the first hour matters. We help you work out what was reached, cut off the access being used, and then decide together what has to be reported and to whom.

Do you test our staff?

Yes, with their knowledge, and not to catch anyone out. We send test messages that look like the real thing, then report the results as a team picture rather than a list of names, so the point is finding out whether the teaching landed.

Will any of this get in the way of how we work?

We aim for the opposite. Controls that make everyday tasks harder get worked around, and a workaround is a new gap. Where a measure would slow your team down, we say so and find another way to cover the risk.
